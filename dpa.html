1. Parties and how this applies

1.1 This Data Processing Agreement ("DPA") is between:

1.2 This DPA forms part of our Terms of Service. It applies automatically when you accept those terms and for as long as we process Customer Personal Information. If this DPA and the Terms conflict about Customer Personal Information, this DPA prevails.

1.3 We offer the app to businesses throughout Australia. Wherever the Customer is located, this DPA operates alongside these Commonwealth laws:

  • the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), including the APPs and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme
  • the Australian Consumer Law

1.4 Australian privacy law doesn't formally use the terms "controller" and "processor". This DPA uses them, in their commonly understood sense, to describe who decides how information is handled (the controller) and who handles it on their behalf (the processor). Both parties remain responsible for their own obligations under the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) where it applies to them.

2. Definitions

  • Customer Personal Information means personal information (as defined in the Privacy Act) in Customer Data that we process for you through the app. It's mainly information about your clients, leads and users.
  • Customer Data has the meaning given in the Terms of Service.
  • APPs means the Australian Privacy Principles in Schedule 1 of the Privacy Act.
  • Data Breach means unauthorised access to, or unauthorised disclosure of, Customer Personal Information, or its loss in circumstances where such access or disclosure is likely, while it's held by us or our subprocessors.
  • Eligible Data Breach has the meaning given in Part IIIC of the Privacy Act (the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme).
  • Subprocessor means a third party we engage that processes Customer Personal Information.

9. Data breach notification

9.1 If we become aware of a Data Breach, we'll notify you without undue delay, and in any case within 72 hours. We'll tell you, as far as we know at the time:

  • what happened and when
  • what kinds of information and roughly how many people are affected
  • the likely consequences
  • what we've done, and recommend you do, to contain it and reduce harm
  • a contact person

9.2 We'll take reasonable steps straight away to contain the breach and reduce any harm. We'll also give you the information and help you reasonably need to assess, within the 30 days the Privacy Act allows, whether it's an Eligible Data Breach.

9.3 If it is an Eligible Data Breach, the parties will agree who prepares the statement to the OAIC and who notifies the affected people, as Part IIIC allows. If we can't agree in time, each party may notify as the Act requires. We won't notify your clients directly without consulting you first, unless the law requires it.

9.4 Our notice of a Data Breach isn't an admission of fault or liability.

10. Requests and assistance

10.1 If someone asks us for access to, or correction of, their information in your workspace, we'll pass the request to you promptly and won't respond to it ourselves unless you ask us to. Most requests can be handled in the app, because you can view, edit, export and delete client records.

10.2 We'll give you reasonable help with privacy impact assessments, regulator enquiries and complaints that relate to our processing.

11. Return and deletion on termination

11.1 You can export Customer Data from the app at any time, including when your workspace is read-only after a failed payment or cancellation.

11.2 We never delete Customer Data automatically when a subscription ends. It stays in your read-only workspace so you can come back, or export it.

11.3 When you ask us in writing to delete it (an owner can email fred@spectrumcoach.com.au):

  • We'll delete the Customer Data, including uploaded documents, from the live system within 30 days.
  • Copies in our database provider's backups expire within the backup retention period, currently up to 7 days.
  • We'll confirm in writing once deletion is done.

11.4 We may keep information that the law requires us to keep, such as billing and tax records. It stays protected under this DPA for as long as we hold it.

This can include a written description of our security measures and our subprocessors' published security reports or certifications. Schedule 2: Security measures

  • Encryption: all connections use HTTPS/TLS, and stored data is encrypted by our hosting provider.
  • Separation between companies: each company's records are kept separate by database row-level security rules. Rules by role limit what each user can see and change.
  • Authentication: passwords are stored as one-way hashes by Supabase Auth. Sign-in sessions use signed tokens.
  • Files: uploaded documents are kept in private storage, available only to authorised users of the right company.
  • Access by our staff: administrator access is limited to the people who need it and is logged in an audit trail. Server keys are kept in the hosting provider's secret storage, never in the app's code.
  • Web enquiries: each enquiry form uses its own key, which is tied to one company and can be limited to one website domain. The endpoint has spam filtering.
  • Payments: card details are handled only by Stripe. We never see or store full card numbers.
  • Resilience: our database provider keeps daily backups.
  • Reviews: we review database security settings regularly and fix issues found by our providers' security checks.
